Cost Controls & Billing Policies

How Power Platform and Copilot Studio consumption bills to Azure, and where the guardrails that stop a runaway agent actually live. · July 2026

The one thing to remember: a billing policy binds to an environment — never to a user. There is no way to “put a user on pay-as-you-go”. You link rooms, not people.

1. The rule people get wrong

Pay-as-you-go is not assigned like a licence. It is attached to environments through a billing policy, and an environment can be linked to exactly one billing policy at a time.

This catches out administrators who expect to enable consumption billing for a particular person or team. The unit of control is the environment, so your environment design is your cost-allocation design. If you need to separate spend between departments, you need separate environments.

2. What a billing policy actually is

A named link between one or more environments and one Azure subscription. Creating one provisions a Power Platform account resource in the Azure subscription’s resource group, and every Power Platform meter bills against that resource.

  • The resource is a hidden type in the Azure portal — enable View hidden types or you will not find it.
  • It can be tagged like any other Azure resource, so spend can be split across teams using resource groups and tags.
  • Pay-as-you-go is available for production and sandbox environments.
  • Deleting the policy in the admin center does not delete the Azure resource — clean that up separately.

A common pattern is one policy per cost centre: the marketing team’s environments billed to their subscription, and a finance team split into separate policies per sub-team so each one’s spend reports cleanly.

If the subscription is missing from the dropdown, it is an Azure problem, not a Power Platform one. You need rights to create resources and register resource providers in that subscription — normally owner or contributor. Administrators frequently waste time looking in the wrong portal for this.

4. Creating one

In the Power Platform admin center: Licensing → Pay-as-you-go plans → New billing plan. Choose the Azure subscription, name the plan, pick the subscription and resource group, select which Power Platform products the meters apply to, then choose a region and the environments to include.

Makers can also enable pay-as-you-go directly from a canvas or model-driven app’s settings without visiting the admin center. Two consequences are worth knowing before anyone does that:

  • Connecting a subscription turns pay-as-you-go on for every app in that environment, not just the one you were looking at.
  • If app passes exist for the environment, they are ignored rather than consumed once pay-as-you-go is active — you may be paying twice without realising.

The Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat special case

Choosing Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat instead of a standard Azure subscription plan automatically provisions an environment of that name, linked to the new plan, which is where the corresponding consumption reporting appears. This is how users without a paid Copilot licence get metered access to agents. Note that administrators cannot delete this environment — it can only be unlinked.

5. Where the cost controls live

Three layers, from coarse to fine. Most organisations only discover the third one after an unpleasant invoice.

  1. Environment allocation — assign prepaid credits per environment under Licensing → Copilot Studio → Manage capacity.
  2. Overage behaviour — when an environment’s allocation reaches zero, choose whether to draw from remaining tenant capacity or bill the overage to a linked pay-as-you-go plan.
  3. Per-agent monthly limits — under Licensing → Copilot Studio → Manage Agents, cap an individual agent whether the environment is prepaid or pay-as-you-go.
This is the answer to “how do we stop one runaway agent running up the bill?” Per-agent limits come with two guardrails: notifications to environment and tenant administrators as an agent approaches its limit, and an optional hard stop that switches the agent off when it reaches one. That is a far better instrument than blunt environment-level controls.

6. Prepaid and pay-as-you-go together

These are not an either/or choice, and layering them is the resilient configuration.

ConfigurationWhat happens when you run out
Prepaid onlyEnforcement bites. Makers are blocked from creating and editing agents, running agents can deactivate, and new agent flow runs are blocked while the parent agent continues serving other interactions.
Pay-as-you-go onlyNothing stops. Usage simply bills to Azure — predictable continuity, less predictable cost.
Prepaid + pay-as-you-go recommendedPrepaid capacity is consumed first and pay-as-you-go absorbs the overage. Continuity without abandoning your committed discount.
Prepaid alone is the configuration that fails at the worst possible moment — typically mid-month, on a business-critical agent, with no warning to the people relying on it. Unused prepaid credits also do not carry over month to month. If an agent matters to the business, put a pay-as-you-go safety net under it.

7. Monitoring what you are spending

Under Licensing → Copilot Studio in the admin center:

  • Summary — active pay-as-you-go plans with month-to-date billed consumption, and prepaid capacity purchased, assigned and consumed.
  • Capacity tab — consumption by product, a daily trend across three months, and the top environments by usage.
  • Environments tab — per-environment credits drawn from prepaid versus billed to pay-as-you-go, whether the environment is in overage, and a per-agent breakdown distinguishing billed from non-billable consumption.

Data retention runs to month-to-date, the previous two months daily, and twelve months of monthly figures — enough for budgeting and licence planning, but export anything you need for longer-term analysis.

8. Turning it off

Either remove the environment from the policy, or delete the policy entirely. The environment reverts to normal behaviour immediately: consumption already incurred still bills, but nothing further does. Remember to remove the orphaned Power Platform account resource in Azure afterwards.

Quick self-check

QuestionWhere it points
Can I put a specific user on pay-as-you-go?No. Billing policies attach to environments only.
How do I separate spend between departments?Separate environments, separate policies, plus Azure resource groups and tags.
How do I stop one agent consuming everything?Per-agent monthly limits with notifications and a hard stop.
Our agent is business-critical — what protects continuity?Prepaid capacity with pay-as-you-go configured as the overage safety net.
Why can’t I see my Azure subscription in the list?Insufficient Azure permissions on that subscription — not a Power Platform issue.
